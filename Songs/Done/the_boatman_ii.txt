The Boatman II


There's a sour note a-blowin' inland off the harbor where the salty ever-after licks the wind
And a preacher bangin' out another hollow, purple sermon binding all the proudest crimson words of sin 
To set me off all righteously to sea
Never minding of the rocking there beneath
That held me in a way Jehovah couldn't dare but dream
While I picked apart the sinews holding him

And there's a glare of reckoning just up off the horizon, and if I had to say I'm barely dreading it
Dreaming of the shore that beckons loudly past the gulf while all the inky black below me fills my pen
The currents and the winds I'll never see
Blowing safely to the warmth of what's deceased
The boathouse on the bank in all its rotting majesty
Calls me, tired, unafraid to sail again

There's a wind a-whippin' 'cross my belly as the haunts upon the shore just wag their digits in the wind
The accusers and the martyrs sitting down there by the boathouse with their upextended pinkies stirring gin
The breeze up off the harbor at the dock
All drenched in a noctournal sulfur fog
The party at the docks just standing grilling their kebabs
Calls me weary of the sea and joining in
