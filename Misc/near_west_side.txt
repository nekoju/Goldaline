My name is Neko Juers, and I, along with my two children, live at The Trellis, a cooperative home located at 921 W 9th St. Having lived in Bloomington since, 2015, one of the features I've loved most about it is the character of its neighborhoods. While the proposed new design guidelines nominally aim to preserve this, they do so in a way that is myopic with respect to the needs of people living in Bloomington; neighborhoods should serve and reflect the needs of their residents and vice-versa and these guidelines do not, save a few very specific interests, because "character" and "appearance" are not synonomous.

The growth and initial development of the Near West-Side Neighborhood was rooted in working class community. That spirit exists at cross-purposes to codifying the necessity of prohibitively expensive architecture and widespread imposition of beaurocratic hurdles at a time when the state of affordable housing in Bloomington is in crisis. The upshot of this plan is a more divided Bloomington, in both the immediate and long terms.

Ultimately this decision is rooted in a value judgement--either toward some uniform ideal of homogeneity of appearance, or accessibility of the Near West-Side Neighborhood as a place to live. I love my community, and I am grateful to be able to call this Neighborhood my family's home, but as one of the people occupying the margins of experience this plan would negatively affect, my home would probably not exist with such a sweeping beaurocratic plan in place as the one that is proposed. In my judgement, a community is not defined by some narrowly defined standard of historicity of its architecture but by the energy of its people manifest by their experiential diversity. There are better, less intrusive, and more equitable paths toward historic preservation, and I oppose this plan for those reasons.
